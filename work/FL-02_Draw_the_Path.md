## 🧠 Section 3: AI Workspace Pressure-Test

### The Prompt Ran:
```text
This is my proof statement: I build interactive frontends that make complex machine learning and LLM flows feel simple, responsive, and tactile. I am proving this to a design lead at a developer-tooling startup, so they will click my Calendly link and book a 15-minute chat to talk about craft, interface design, and frontends.

This is my sitemap:
1. Landing Page (Home / Claim): Immediate declaration of what I build, a primary CTA to my Calendly, and a list of my live project demos.
2. The Work (Interactive Demos): A single workspace hosting 2-3 live, clickable demos (e.g., the NLP classifier running live with liquid glass UI) so they can test the responsiveness immediately.
3. Inside the Craft (Writing): Breakdown of how I resolved the backend complexity, latency UX, and frontend states.
4. About & Contact: A brief background on my MSc AI + UI/UX design path, with a direct Calendly booking widget.

Does this sitemap walk my one person from landing, to believing me, to taking my one action, and does it actually prove my claim? Tell me which pages earn their place, which are wasted, and what is missing. Be specific, not encouraging.
```

### The AI Output (Pressure-Test Critique):
> 1. **High Friction / Click Fatigue:** You are asking a busy Design Lead to click multiple times. If your landing page lists the demos, and "The Work" is a separate page, you are hiding the proof. Design Leads at developer-tooling companies have zero patience; if they don't see live, tactile proof within 5 seconds above or below the fold on the landing page, they will leave.
> 2. **Wasted Pages:** A separate "About & Contact" page is a waste. Your only desired action is a Calendly booking. This booking widget and a brief bio should be embedded directly at the bottom of a single scrolling page.
> 3. **Contextual Gaps:** "Inside the Craft" as a standalone page separates the explanation from the interaction. The explanation of *how* you solved latency UX should sit directly adjacent to the interactive demo itself, perhaps as an expandable code/design drawer.

## 🛠️ Section 4: What I Will Change

Based on the pressure-test analysis, I will make the following adjustments to my portfolio design:

*   **Change:** I am collapsing the 4-page sitemap into a **single, high-impact landing page** with one sub-page.
    *   **The Landing Page** will host the hero section, the live interactive demos (no separate "Work" page), my bio, and the Calendly booking widget.
    *   The only external click will be to open a detailed write-up page (**Inside the Craft**) for a specific case study, explaining the technical and design details.
*   This keeps the user journey extremely short, showcases my interactive work immediately, and channels all traffic straight to the Calendly link.
